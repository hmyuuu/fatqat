    Qudit gates¶

    These gates are defined for local dimensions d >= 2; the table gives any additional dimension rules. add records the operation, and the backend checks support when the program runs. Integer power values are reduced modulo the relevant target dimension, so negative and oversized values are valid.

    Unary gates accept one scalar target or one RegisterView. Two-target gates accept scalars or two compatible views and zip their members.

    Qudit gates

    Value or constructor Targets and constraints Basis action
    Shift (power) One scalar or view; any d >= 2 \|k> -> \|(k + power) mod d>. Shift(1) is X for d=2.
    Clock (power) One scalar or view; any d >= 2 \|k> -> omega**(k*power)\|k>, omega=exp(2*pi*i/d). Clock(1) is Z for d=2.
    Sum Scalar or view pair with equal dimensions \|i,j> -> \|i,(i+j) mod d>. It is CX for d=2.
    SwapLevels (j, k) One scalar or view; 0 <= j,k < d and j != k Exchanges \|j> and \|k> and fixes every other level.
    Fourier One scalar or view; any d >= 2 \|j> -> sum(exp(2*pi*i*j*k/d)\|k>) / sqrt(d). It is H for d=2.
    InverseFourier One scalar or view; any d >= 2 Conjugate transpose of Fourier; uses the negative exponent.
    SubspaceRX (theta, (j, k)) One scalar or view; two distinct in-range levels With c=cos(theta/2), s=sin(theta/2): \|j> -> c\|j>-i*s\|k> and \|k> -> -i*s\|j>+c\|k>.
    SubspaceRY (theta, (j, k)) One scalar or view; two distinct in-range levels \|j> -> c\|j>+s\|k> and \|k> -> -s\|j>+c\|k>. Reversing (j, k) reverses the rotation.
    SubspaceRZ (theta, (j, k)) One scalar or view; two distinct in-range levels \|j> gains exp(-i*theta/2) and \|k> gains exp(i*theta/2). Reversing the pair reverses the rotation.
    CClock (power) Scalar or view pair; dimensions may differ \|i,j> gains omega**(i*j*power) using the target's omega=exp(2*pi*i/d_target). It is CZ for two qubits and power 1.

    Level pairs must contain distinct, non-negative integers. Equality and negativity are checked at construction, and the target dimension is checked when the operation is added. Sum requires equal control and target dimensions; the backend rejects a mismatch when the program runs.

    Matrix definitions¶

    The matrices below act on column vectors. For one-qudit gates, rows and columns use the computational-basis order \(\lvert 0\rangle,\lvert 1\rangle,\ldots,\lvert d-1\rangle\).

    Shift and Clock¶

    For Shift(power=p) and Clock(power=p), let \(\omega_d=\exp(2\pi i/d)\). Their general operators are

    \[ X_d^p = \sum_{k=0}^{d-1} \left\lvert (k+p)\bmod d \right\rangle\!\left\langle k\right\rvert, \qquad Z_d^p = \sum_{k=0}^{d-1} \omega_d^{pk}\left\lvert k\right\rangle\!\left\langle k\right\rvert. \]

    Sum¶

    Sum takes operands as (control, target). For equal dimension d, the control is the local most-significant factor:

    \[ \operatorname{SUM}_d = \sum_{i,j=0}^{d-1} \left\lvert i,(i+j)\bmod d\right\rangle \!\left\langle i,j\right\rvert. \]

    SwapLevels¶

    For distinct levels \(j\) and \(k\), the general operator is

    \[ S_{j,k} = I - \lvert j\rangle\!\langle j\rvert - \lvert k\rangle\!\langle k\rvert + \lvert j\rangle\!\langle k\rvert + \lvert k\rangle\!\langle j\rvert. \]

    Fourier transforms¶

    With \(\omega_d=\exp(2\pi i/d)\), Fourier and InverseFourier are

    \[ F_d = \frac{1}{\sqrt d}\sum_{j,k=0}^{d-1} \omega_d^{jk}\lvert k\rangle\!\langle j\rvert, \qquad F_d^{-1}=F_d^\dagger = \frac{1}{\sqrt d}\sum_{j,k=0}^{d-1} \omega_d^{-jk}\lvert k\rangle\!\langle j\rvert. \]

    Subspace rotations¶

    Let \(c=\cos(\theta/2)\) and \(s=\sin(\theta/2)\). For the ordered level pair subspace=(j, k), the general operators are

    \[ \begin{aligned} R_X^{(j,k)}(\theta) &= I + (c-1)(\lvert j\rangle\!\langle j\rvert +\lvert k\rangle\!\langle k\rvert) -is(\lvert j\rangle\!\langle k\rvert +\lvert k\rangle\!\langle j\rvert), \\ R_Y^{(j,k)}(\theta) &= I + (c-1)(\lvert j\rangle\!\langle j\rvert +\lvert k\rangle\!\langle k\rvert) -s\lvert j\rangle\!\langle k\rvert +s\lvert k\rangle\!\langle j\rvert, \\ R_Z^{(j,k)}(\theta) &= I + (e^{-i\theta/2}-1)\lvert j\rangle\!\langle j\rvert +(e^{i\theta/2}-1)\lvert k\rangle\!\langle k\rvert. \end{aligned} \]

    CClock¶

    CClock(power=p) takes operands as (control, target). If their dimensions are \(d_c\) and \(d_t\), the control is the local most-significant factor, \(\omega_t=\exp(2\pi i/d_t)\), and

    \[ \operatorname{CClock}_{d_c,d_t}^{(p)} = \sum_{i=0}^{d_c-1}\sum_{j=0}^{d_t-1} \omega_t^{ijp}\lvert i,j\rangle\!\langle i,j\rvert. \]

    API reference¶

    Common operation properties are documented on the Operations overview.

    Shift dataclass ¶

    Bases: Operation

    Cyclically shift one subsystem's computational-basis level.

    On a target of dimension d, |k> maps to |(k + power) mod d>. Negative and oversized integer powers are valid and equivalent modulo d. Shift(1) on a qubit is X.

    Parameters:

    • power (int) –

      Integer cyclic shift amount.

    Clock dataclass ¶

    Bases: Operation

    Apply a dimension-dependent phase to each basis level.

    On a target of dimension d, |k> gains phase omega**(k*power), where omega = exp(2*pi*i/d). Negative and oversized integer powers are valid and equivalent modulo d. Clock(1) on a qubit is Z.

    Parameters:

    • power (int) –

      Integer phase power.

    SwapLevels dataclass ¶

    Bases: Operation

    Exchange two basis levels and leave every other level unchanged.

    The gate is Hermitian and self-inverse. On a qubit, SwapLevels(0, 1) is X.

    Parameters:

    • j (int) –

      First non-negative level index.

    • k (int) –

      Distinct second non-negative level index.

    Raises:

    • ValueError –

      At construction if the indices are equal or negative, or from fatqat.Program.add if either index is outside the target's 0 <= index < dim range.

    SubspaceRX dataclass ¶

    Bases: Operation

    Apply RX inside two selected levels and leave other levels unchanged.

    In the ordered pair (j, k), j has RX's |0> role and k has its |1> role. SubspaceRX(theta, (0, 1)) on a qubit is RX with the same theta.

    Parameters:

    • theta (float | Parameter) –

      Numeric angle in radians, or a fatqat.Parameter to bind before execution.

    • subspace (tuple[int, int]) –

      Tuple of exactly two distinct, non-negative integer level indices in (|0>, |1>) role order.

    Raises:

    • ValueError –

      At construction if subspace does not contain exactly two values, its indices are equal, or an index is negative; or from fatqat.Program.add if an index is outside the target dimension.

    SubspaceRY dataclass ¶

    Bases: Operation

    Apply RY inside two selected levels and leave other levels unchanged.

    In the ordered pair (j, k), j has RY's |0> role and k has its |1> role, so reversing the pair reverses the rotation direction. SubspaceRY(theta, (0, 1)) on a qubit is RY with the same theta.

    Parameters:

    • theta (float | Parameter) –

      Numeric angle in radians, or a fatqat.Parameter to bind before execution.

    • subspace (tuple[int, int]) –

      Tuple of exactly two distinct, non-negative integer level indices in (|0>, |1>) role order.

    Raises:

    • ValueError –

      At construction if subspace does not contain exactly two values, its indices are equal, or an index is negative; or from fatqat.Program.add if an index is outside the target dimension.

    SubspaceRZ dataclass ¶

    Bases: Operation

    Apply RZ inside two selected levels and leave other levels unchanged.

    In the ordered pair (j, k), j gains phase exp(-i*theta/2) and k gains exp(i*theta/2). Reversing the pair reverses the rotation. SubspaceRZ(theta, (0, 1)) on a qubit is RZ with the same theta.

    Parameters:

    • theta (float | Parameter) –

      Numeric angle in radians, or a fatqat.Parameter to bind before execution.

    • subspace (tuple[int, int]) –

      Tuple of exactly two distinct, non-negative integer level indices in (|0>, |1>) role order.

    Raises:

    • ValueError –

    CClock dataclass ¶

    Bases: Operation

    Apply a control-level-dependent Clock phase to a target qudit.

    Targets are (control, target). On basis state |i, j>, the phase is omega**(i*j*power), where omega = exp(2*pi*i/target_dimension). Control and target dimensions may differ. Negative and oversized integer powers are valid and equivalent modulo the target dimension. On two qubits, CClock(1) is CZ.

    Parameters:

    • power (int) –

      Integer phase power.
